# RPG, Survival, Companions & Dungeons

CrownFold's open-world layer is a home and a story, **not just a war-rift lobby**. The player can ignore an operation and wander the countryside, collect resources, earn money, solve puzzles, rest at camp, forge equipment, accept quests or recruit companions to cross chronology with them.

## Currently implemented

- **Third-person shooter:** six firearms with distinct damage, range, magazines and firing behavior; grenades, resupply, orbital strike, dodge, sprint and crouch/stealth.
- **Survival:** hunger, thirst, stamina, health, shields, harvestable resources and crafting consumables. Field shelter, garden, refinery, turret, barricade, beacon and shield bastion structures.
- **RPG progression:** levels, 18 rankable perks in Warfare / Survival / Mastery / Command / Arcana, rarity-based equipment and crafting recipes. Six additional active Rift abilities: Blink, Nova, Stasis, Ghost Veil, Wisp Swarm, War Chant.
- **Quest layer:** 13 scripted quest definitions inherited from the RPG prototype, repeatable bounties, three main factions, conversations, a story-allegiance conclusion, pickpocketing, fines, safecracking and merchants.
- **Companions:** two original named recruitables plus cross-era recruitable witnesses who follow you through Advanced Rifts. They participate in the persistent small squad and continue receiving RTS move/follow/assault orders.
- **Exploration:** eight prior medieval landmarks, three original dungeon instances, three survey sites per era (15), rare loot, persistent discoveries, and caravan travel for surveyed sites.
- **Dungeon gameplay:** Mirror Maw Vault, Cathedral of Roots, Drowned Archive. Each has chambers, two relays/sigils, enemy guardians, boss and relic treasure. War campaigns and open-world exploration are distinct modes.

- **Aurelia the Unwritten (1278):** An interpreter who has met rivals before they existed.
- **Hush-of-the-Thicket (12,400 BCE):** A hunter whose unrecorded instinct breaks computer predictions.
- **Ogara of the Bone Sea (12,400 BCE):** A mammoth-route trader and guide.
- **Madame Brasswell (1889):** A union engineer who dismantles starships with screwdrivers.
- **Engren the Clockmaker (1889):** Heavy recruit with centuries-old unnetworked machinery.
- **Iris Null (2086):** Street courier profiting from missing borders.
- **Captain Voss (2086):** A former shock trooper who learned humility from swordsmen.
- **Theorem-9 (8420):** Dissident predictive mind, a merchant and philosophical witness.
- **Ell of No Tomorrow (8420):** A healer who refuses predetermined futures.

Their present browser functions are dialogue lore, merchant interaction and recruitment for suitable characters. Large branching personal quest lines for them are not yet written or implemented.

## Design vision for a living world

Every century should contain towns with economies, schedules, festivals, crime laws and politics, factions with territory disputes, living companions with personal quest chains, static authored dungeons plus seeded remixable adventures, settlements that attract new citizens, rewards that cross-pollinate between shooter, explorer and strategist playstyles, dynamic caravans transporting weapons between time periods, and real environmental hazards. A merchant in 1278 might sell potatoes grown in 8420; a farmer can become a vital ally without ever wielding a weapon.

The game should reward curiosity as much as conquest: historical discoveries unlock civilian solutions, settlements can resolve conflicts peacefully, a defeated commander might become a companion, and a small side quest might destabilize a heavily fortified campaign five centuries later.

## Constraints

All content in this section is original fictional material. Similarities to sandbox RPG, RTS and extraction shooter genres are gameplay references, not licensed recreations. The browser prototype is substantially smaller than a released AAA RPG in quest quantity, authored art, open-world density and animation.
